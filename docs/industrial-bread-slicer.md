# Industrial Bread Slicer — Stage 4

Registry ID: `craftdistrict:bakery_bread_slicer`. One static, horizontally
rotatable block and item. No functional machinery, interaction, inventory,
Block Entity, entity renderer, animation or tick callback.

## Editable geometry

`tools/generate_bakery_bread_slicer.py` owns named machine parts, loaf/slice
placement, four 16×16 pixel textures, block/item resources, loot and the generated
`BreadSlicerShapes.java`. Run it with Python 3. Other generators and existing
bakery models are never regenerated by this script.

North (-Z) is the output/front. The low charcoal chassis has four feet and a
brushed-steel feed bed. Two columns flank an open bread throat beneath a sloped
protective cover. Guides separate the front output and rear insertion areas.
Side ventilation, a recessed side panel, rear maintenance framing and a bridge
seam finish the machine. The control panel has a green start button, red stop
button, adjustment knob/index and an asymmetric indicator with outward UVs.
There are no exposed cutting blades.

The rear whole loaf has a 5.25×3.75×5.5-unit octagonal profile and golden crust.
Five front slices are each 5×3.625×0.65 units, with 0.375-unit gaps and slight
lateral staggering. Each slice has a pale crumb face, dark crust edges and a
rounded silhouette. Six broad cuboids per bread profile avoid tiny staircase
geometry. Bread rests on the bed at Y=3. All geometry stays inside one 16-unit
cell. Face maps, rotations and texture references are validated before export;
fully hidden elements are discarded.

Final geometry: 66 visible cuboids / 358 faces, approximately 14 units wide,
12.008 units high and 14.5 units deep, including feet, bread and controls.

Collision and selection use the same 17 conservative envelopes, cached in four
rotations. Rounded bread corners and spaces underneath feet are intentionally
approximated by broader bounds; this is furniture collision, not mesh collision.
The output slices retain separate envelopes and gaps.

## Placement and compatibility

`BreadSlicerBlock` reuses `CashRegisterBlock`'s solid upper-face support checks,
placement validation, support-loss removal and collision dispatch. The only
change to the cash-register implementation removes `final` from its class
declaration; its methods, shapes and assets are unchanged. The slicer supplies
its own cached shape and Minecraft 1.21 codec.

Use a full solid surface with an upward sturdy face. The display counter has
full-block support on both halves and a surface at Y=16, so the slicer occupies
the separate block cell above either half. A floor or suitable top slab can also
support it. Ordinary Minecraft placement rejects an occupied, nonreplaceable
destination; decorations cannot share a block cell.

The approved dough preparation table has its working surface at Y=12.5 and
fixed props above it. It does not provide a sturdy top face at the cell boundary.
Placement is rejected; lowering a slicer into the same cell would intersect that
table and its props. A future clear, full-height equipment table could support
these machines without changing this placement architecture. No such change is
part of Stage 4.

The block uses normal single-block breaking and one-item loot. Supporting-block
removal follows the same vanilla neighbor-shape removal as the cash register.
No linked-part removal or new multi-block mechanics are necessary.

## Verification and previews

Run `tools/test_generate_bakery_bread_slicer.py` for six static checks covering
legal geometry and textures, separated bread/support contact, shape rotation and
coverage, outward indicator UVs and exported states/item/loot.

Build all targets from the repository root:

```powershell
.\gradlew.bat :forge:1.20.1:build :fabric:1.20.1:build :neoforge:1.21.1:build :fabric:1.21.1:build --no-parallel --console=plain
```

Regenerate deterministic previews with:

```powershell
python tools/preview_bakery_model.py bakery_bread_slicer --views --rear --top
```

The front, side, rear, isometric, top and detailed previews are under ignored
`build/asset-previews/`. These depict exported JSON geometry; they are not
screenshots of Minecraft and do not simulate its full lighting or ambient
occlusion. Verification reports and a temporary read-only resource probe are
under ignored `build/bread-slicer-verification/`, never inside distributable JARs.

For manual review, launch any of:

```powershell
.\gradlew.bat :forge:1.20.1:runClient
.\gradlew.bat :fabric:1.20.1:runClient
.\gradlew.bat :neoforge:1.21.1:runClient
.\gradlew.bat :fabric:1.21.1:runClient
```

Then open a test world and check:

- Find Industrial Bread Slicer in the CraftDistrict Creative tab.
- Place on a full block and both display-counter halves, in all four directions.
- Check rotation of collision/selection, walking around it, and bread readability.
- Check occupied-cell obstruction and unsupported placement rejection.
- Break in Survival (one item) and Creative (no item duplication).
- Remove its support and confirm normal removal/drop behavior.
- Save/reload and verify state and appearance persist.
- Inspect controls, seam edges and loaf/slice appearance under actual lighting.

Client startup/model baking verification does not verify these world behaviors.
Stop after Stage 4 for the user's in-game review.
